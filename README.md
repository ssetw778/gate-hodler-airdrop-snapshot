# Gate HODLer Airdrop: How 1 GT Unlocks Free Token Rounds, What the Snapshot Math Actually Rewards, and Which VIP Tier Raises the Cap

Three questions bring people to this page. Is the HODLer Airdrop genuinely free, how much GT do I need to hold, and is parking capital in Gate's own token just to collect airdrops worth it. The short answers: yes, one GT, and it depends entirely on how long you're willing to leave that GT sitting there.

That last part is where most write-ups get vague, so let's do the mechanics first.

## What the Gate HODLer Airdrop actually is

Gate runs a recurring distribution for holders of GT (GateToken), the platform token and the native asset of the GateChain mainnet. A project supplies a pool of its own tokens. Users who have completed identity verification and hold at least one GT register on the round's page, and the pool gets split according to GT holdings.

The important word is "hold." You are not swapping GT for the new token. Gate's own FAQ states plainly that participating funds are not deducted after a round ends — the GT stays in your account and only functions as the basis for eligibility and reward weight.

Scale is not trivial. Gate's HODLer Airdrop landing page has shown cumulative distributions of roughly **$320 million** across about **340 rounds** and **more than 12 million participants**. Those counters move, so treat them as a snapshot rather than a fixed number.

Rounds also arrive quickly. Running through Gate's own round history for 2026: FOGO in mid-January, ACU, 3KDS, then CRYPTOBURG and PACT in late February, IDOS and NEXI in early March, WorldLand (#338) at the end of March, ST (#339) in April, Zest Protocol (#340) in May, and RAIN in late June. That's broadly one round every week or two.

## The rule people misread: your average, not your balance

This is the single most misread part of the product. You do not get weighted by whatever GT you happen to be holding when you click join.

> Tokens you receive = (your average hourly GT holdings during the round ÷ all participants' combined average hourly holdings during the round) × total airdrop pool

The system takes multiple random snapshots inside each hour, averages them into an hourly figure, then averages those hourly figures across the whole round. What lands in your account is based on that final average.

Two consequences follow. Buying a large stack of GT right before a round closes does not retroactively upgrade your whole position, because earlier hours were already recorded at a lower balance. Selling or moving GT out midway through a round quietly drags your average down and shrinks the payout.

One genuinely useful detail, and it's less known than it should be: registering a few hours after a round opens isn't fatal. Gate's current mechanism can look back and count holdings from earlier in the round, so the clock doesn't start at your click. You still have to register during the round window.

Averages below 1 GT get nothing. Rewards are calculated and distributed within one hour after a round ends, and you can find them under Earn → HODLer Airdrop → History.

## What qualifying actually costs you

The floor is one GT plus completed verification. Gate's own research post from mid-August 2026 put GT at about **$6.70**, which makes the minimum stake small enough to be an afterthought. The number that matters is how much GT you're willing to leave parked, because that's what produces the payout.

Two rules to know before you start:

- **Main accounts only.** Institutions and sub-accounts are explicitly excluded from the HODLer Airdrop.
- **Bonus credit exists.** On rounds tagged with an "Airdrop Bonus" label, holding GT in Simple Earn's fixed-term product adds bonus credit to your eligible holdings. If your standard holdings have already hit your personal cap, the bonus counts for nothing — 0 additional reward.

For VIP evaluation, GT sitting in spot, margin and earn accounts all counts toward the 14-day average. So GT earning yield in Simple Earn is not dead weight for tier purposes. ## VIP level is what decides your cap

Holdings past a certain point stop counting. Each round sets its own cap for VIP 0–VIP 4, and higher VIP tiers get raised limits. The Zest Protocol round is the clearest published example:

The caps are per round, not a permanent product rule. In the WorldLand round, VIP 0–4 was capped at 1,000 GT. In the Crypto Burger round, that same band sat at 5,000 GT. So the honest way to plan is: check the specific round's announcement before deciding how much GT to buy, because the ceiling you're buying against may be lower than you expect.

This is also the reason a "hold a few thousand GT and farm airdrops" strategy has a natural ceiling. Past your cap, extra GT costs capital and adds nothing to that round.

## How to join a round, step by step

1. Log in to your main Gate account and complete identity verification.
2. Hold at least 1 GT. Spot wallet is fine; higher tiers benefit from GT that also sits in earn products.
3. Go to Earn → HODLer Airdrop and pick a round marked as live.
4. Read the round's own announcement before you commit more capital — caps, pool size and end time change every time.
5. Click Join Now and leave your GT in place through the round. Don't shuffle it out and back in.
6. Check Earn → HODLer Airdrop → History an hour or so after the round closes.

If you're doing this from zero, 👉 [the sign-up and verification path starts here](https://bit.ly/GateVIP) — verification takes a day or so in most cases, so it's worth doing ahead of a round you care about rather than during one.

## HODLer Airdrop versus CandyDrop and Launchpool

Gate groups all three under its launch section, and people mix them up constantly.

- **CandyDrop** is task-driven. You click Join Now and complete tasks — trading, deposits, invites. Separate ledger, separate eligibility.
- **Launchpool** is staking. You commit assets (GT, USDT, BTC, ETH or a project token, depending on the pool) and earn by share of the pool.
- **HODLer Airdrop** is holdings-only. No lock-up, no tasks. Hold GT, register, get weighted by your average.

Doing CandyDrop tasks does not create HODLer eligibility, and holding GT doesn't complete CandyDrop tasks. They run side by side and don't substitute for one another. If you have capital you can stake, Launchpool is the more active use of it; if your edge is simply holding GT, that's this product.

## The risks, unsentimentally

The airdrop is free. The position isn't.

GT has a market price, and you're holding it for the duration of a round — a few days to a couple of weeks, and longer if you're rolling from one round to the next. If GT drops 15% while you're farming a distribution, the loss on the position can easily exceed the value of the tokens you collected. The Gate Learn page on this product says the same thing in its own words: the P&L on your GT position can outweigh the airdrop.

Second, newly distributed tokens are volatile by nature. Receiving tokens is not the same as receiving a known amount of money, and some rounds unlock 100% at listing, which means the price discovery happens while you hold.

Third, the rules really do change per round. Caps, VIP treatment, bonus eligibility and pool sizes all shift. Any guide, including this one, is describing a mechanism rather than a promise about the next round.

## Common questions

**Does joining deduct my GT?** No. Under the standard mechanism, GT determines eligibility and reward weight; it isn't exchanged for the new token.

**If I only hold 1 GT, do I still get tokens?** Gate's FAQ states that users holding at least 1 GT can receive the airdrop, provided they hold at least 1 GT at the snapshot times. The amount scales with your average position, so 1 GT gets you a share, not a meaningful one.

**Can I be in several rounds at once?** Yes, as long as you meet each round's requirements and hold enough GT at each round's snapshot times.

**Can I use a sub-account?** Not currently. Main accounts only.

**What if the tokens never show up?** Check your eligibility and your GT balance at the snapshot times first, then contact support.

The HODLer Airdrop is a low-friction way to get exposure to new listings if you were going to hold GT anyway. The mechanics reward patience: keep the position steady across the round, register early, and check whether the round's cap actually applies to you before deciding that a bigger stack is worth buying.
